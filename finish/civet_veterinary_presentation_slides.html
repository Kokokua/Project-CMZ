STEREOTYPIC PACING ETHOLOGY & BIOMARKERS

Re-Evaluating Pacing: From Presumed Distress to Clinical Health Biomarker

Captive Solitary Carnivores Channel Innate Appetitive Patrol Drives into Circuit Locomotion

Traditional Stereotypy Stigma

Repetitive pacing was historically branded as an intractable sign of psychological suffering. Zoo visitors often assume an animal pacing at night is severely distressed.

However, solitary viverrids in the wild patrol 1.5–3 km² nightly. In captivity, this motor drive persists innately.
Modern Clinical Ethology

Contemporary ethology (Mason et al.) recognizes bounded pacing as an adaptive homeostatic outlet for appetitive locomotion when live prey is absent.

Key Diagnostic Question: Is the pacing bounded in time? Is it accompanied by self-harm? Does it prevent sleep?
The AI Biomarker Breakthrough

Our system converts pacing into an objective musculoskeletal screen. Maintaining 1.29 m/s across 2+ km proves intact joint mobility, tendon elasticity, and cardiopulmonary vigor.

Early warning for osteoarthritis: lap cycle time degradation or asymmetrical limp velocity.
